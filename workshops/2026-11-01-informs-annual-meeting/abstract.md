# SEFOP: a Software Engineering Framework for Optimization Programs

Decision-support software — systems that use mathematical optimization to help make better decisions —
faces a known gap between the mathematical models and the engineering quality of the software that
delivers their results. Operations Research (OR) scientists are trained deeply in optimization theory, but
rarely in the software engineering practices that make their models robust, maintainable, and deployable in
practice. Vidoni (2021) formally identifies this as a structural deficit, proposing "OR Engineering" as a missing
dimension of OR practice.

The software development lifecycle has evolved significantly over the years to address this problem, both
with principles and tools: the Agile paradigm established changeability as a primary design goal, supported
by modularity, testing, and CI/CD now as the state-of-the-art technique for modern software. However, these
techniques have not yet permeated OR Engineering — the community that builds decision-support software
continues to operate largely outside the engineering culture the broader software world has spent decades
establishing.

SEFOP — the Software Engineering Framework for Optimization — addresses these challenges through two
complementary layers: a freely accessible training curriculum covering software engineering principles, and
pre-built software templates that encode those principles into a working structure practitioners can
immediately adapt.

The urgency of SEFOP is amplified by the rapid adoption of AI coding assistants. Although they provide a
short-term speed boost, recent research has shown that these tools act as amplifiers: they magnify existing
engineering strengths and weaknesses equally. SEFOP ensures OR teams are prepared to capture the
benefits rather than compound the risks.
